Discovering Plants: An Interactive Multimedia Journey Through South African Life Sciences

Description of performance requirement

The Multimedia eLearning Environment performance calls for a multimedia learning environment that combines instructional resources, learner interaction, varied assessment approaches and opportunities for collaborative learning.

My performance

Context and purpose

The South African Life Sciences curriculum includes Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS), yet teachers may have limited resources to connect culturally situated plant knowledge with formal scientific concepts. I developed Discovering Plants as a self-navigated interactive PowerPoint resource to explore these connections through familiar South African examples.

What I developed

The 16-slide experience begins with learning objectives and a topic menu. It includes pathways on South African biomes and biodiversity, plant adaptations, medicinal plants such as Aloe ferox, African potato and wild garlic, plant nutrition, and conservation. Learners are prompted to compare Indigenous Knowledge with scientific knowledge and reflect on the preservation of IKS. The module includes video/resource links, a five-question quiz challenge, and a closing summary and resource list.

What the artifact demonstrates

The outcome is a functioning multimedia instructional product—not an experimental finding about learner achievement. The menu, Next/Previous controls and topic choices allow visitors to explore material nonlinearly. The quiz offers a knowledge check, while the reflective question creates space for interpretation and discussion. The design is related to my literature review identifying a need for more culturally responsive multimedia in Life Sciences.

Implications and next steps

The module offers a starting point for teaching plant science alongside locally relevant knowledge. In a classroom pilot I would gather teacher and learner feedback, test the reliability of links and interactions on different devices, add opportunities for collaboration and feedback, and check scientific and cultural representations with suitable experts and knowledge holders. Learning gains cannot be claimed before such evaluation.

Professional reflection

Creating this module required me to decide which forms of interactivity genuinely help someone learn, rather than adding media for appearance. I had to translate a broad research problem into a manageable sequence of scientific examples, choices and checks for understanding. It also reminded me that the respectful representation of Indigenous Knowledge requires care beyond selecting relevant pictures or examples.

Performance alignment with AECT standards

Indicator descriptions below are concise summaries of the AECT 2012 standards. Each connection is tied to evidence in this performance; proposed next steps are not claimed as completed outcomes.

AECT 1.1 — Creating: Creation of instructional materials and learning environments using systematic approaches

I created an organised learning environment rather than an ordinary slide lecture: learning objectives, a selectable menu, differentiated topic pathways and an end-of-module knowledge check can be inspected in the PowerPoint.

AECT 2.1 — Creating: Application of content pedagogy when creating technology-supported instruction

I connected Life Sciences content—biomes, adaptations, nutrition and plant biology—to a pedagogical design inviting comparison of Indigenous and scientific accounts of medicinal plants.

AECT 3.2 — Using: Sound selection of resources using learning principles and effective practices

I chose interactive PowerPoint for branching navigation and combined concise text, images, links and learner prompts. This reflects the multimedia-design reasoning examined in my companion literature review.

AECT 3.3 — Assessing/Evaluating: Use of multiple assessment strategies to improve learning

The five-question Quiz Yourself section and the IKS reflection prompt provide two different checks on learner thinking. They are designed assessments, not evidence that learner performance has already improved.

AECT 2.5 — Ethics: Design and selection of media attentive to multicultural diversity

The focus on indigenous medicinal plants and IKS makes cultural relevance an explicit design consideration rather than an isolated mention of local knowledge.

Performance alignment with ISTE Standards for Coaches

These are evidence-based connections to selected ISTE Coaches indicators. The standards describe coaching competencies; my coursework establishes the aspects I designed or researched, while activities requiring actual coaching or evaluated impact are identified as future work.

ISTE 4.4.d — Model instructional design principles

In Discovering Plants, I structured an interactive menu, short topic-based sections, visuals and a knowledge check. These are tangible examples of using multimedia design principles in a learner-facing module. The submitted PowerPoint demonstrates my design decisions; modeling them with other educators would be a subsequent coaching activity.

ISTE 4.4.b — Support effective assessments

The final quiz on photosynthesis, fynbos and Indigenous Knowledge offers a way to check understanding after learners explore the activities. It demonstrates assessment design, though the slide deck does not establish that I analyzed learner responses or provided individualized feedback.
