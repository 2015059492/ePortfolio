Research journey

Research foundation.

My research has progressed from reviewing how AI is used in science classrooms to examining whether future Life Sciences teachers are prepared to integrate it. Both projects inform the questions guiding my doctoral study.

B.Ed. Honours · University of the Witwatersrand · 2024

Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Secondary School Science Teaching: A Systematic Review of the Research 2000–2024

This systematic review synthesised findings from ten studies examining the use and effectiveness of AI in secondary school science education. It considered intelligent tutoring systems, adaptive learning platforms and virtual laboratories, as well as reported outcomes for teaching and learning.

Findings and implications. The reviewed research reported potential benefits for personalised learning, learner engagement and conceptual understanding. It also revealed persistent challenges involving teacher preparedness, infrastructure and data privacy. The study recommended teacher professional development, equitable resource allocation and further investigation of long-term effects in different schooling contexts.

M.Ed. Science Education · University of the Witwatersrand · 2026

Life Sciences Pre-Service Teachers’ Perceptions and Preparedness for Integration of AI in One South African University

This qualitative exploratory case study investigated the experiences and views of 38 pre-service Life Sciences teachers, using open-ended questionnaires and semi-structured interviews. The analysis was informed by the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge (TPACK) framework.

Findings and implications. Participants generally recognised the value of AI for explaining and visualising complex Life Sciences concepts, but much of their familiarity was self-directed. A knowledge–practice gap emerged, along with ethical concerns and infrastructural constraints. The study recommends structured, subject-specific AI preparation within Initial Teacher Education and further investigation of classroom integration.

Current research · University of the Witwatersrand

Current doctoral research.

AI-Powered Virtual Laboratories in South African Life Sciences Education: A Design-Based Study of Pedagogical Integration and Learner Outcomes

My PhD focuses on how AI-powered virtual laboratories can be integrated into South African Life Sciences teaching. The planned design-based study will investigate how teachers use these environments in classroom practice and how features such as adaptive feedback and guided investigation might support learners' conceptual understanding and engagement.

The work is particularly concerned with the realities of schools that have limited laboratory resources or digital infrastructure. Through iterative design and collaboration with teachers, I aim to examine both pedagogical possibilities and practical constraints. As this is ongoing doctoral research, learning outcomes and effectiveness are questions to be investigated, not established results.
